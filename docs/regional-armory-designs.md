# Regional Village Armory design specifications

Architecture specification for the compact storehouse and supply yard in
Desert, Savanna, Taiga, Snowy, deep ocean, Jungle, and Swamp settings. These
fourteen designs extend the [Plains Armory previews](armory-preview.md).
They now have [dimensioned drawings and coordinate schedules](regional-armory-drawings.md)
and [furnished preview templates](regional-armory-preview.md). The separate
[integration packs](regional-armory-integration.md) connect them to village
roads. This specification records the design intent; the preview and
integration guides describe native refinements and packaged-game validation.

The intended character is **rustic village defense buildings, built in the
local architectural style, with practical fortifications and believable
interiors**. Each pair shares its region's materials and joinery with the
[Barracks](regional-barracks-designs.md), while reading as a supply building:
sheltered deliveries, sturdy storage, a bench for sorting equipment, and an
obvious entrance. Change the silhouette and arrangement along with the palette.

## Common program and dimensions

The [Armory design brief](armory-designs.md) defines the functional contract.
Each building has one Village Armory, one crafting table, decorative shelves
and crates, a work surface, a short rest bench, and an entrance service-sentry
position. The supply yard provides more working and passing space with the
same supply function. It grants no additional stock or garrison.

Keep the unrotated Armory facing north, with a clear 3 × 3 service area
immediately north of it and room at its interior sides. Its block and the
space above it are reserved for the actual store. Shelves and counters belong
elsewhere. Principal routes retain two clear floor cells across and three air
blocks above the walking surface. Paired wooden doors are the existing
two-block-high exception; their leaves reduce the usable width when open.
Place the sentry beside the approach, with space for guards to walk past.

Use full blocks beneath indoor floors and principal deck routes. Slabs serve
threshold transitions and architectural trim. Open water, carpet, snow layers,
trapdoor bridges, and loose decoration do not form required route surfaces.
The only exterior doors lead onto supported arrival space. Roofs and attics
remain unoccupied; roof-access stairs and ladders are outside these designs.

Dimensions below are **width × depth × height**, including eaves, foundations,
rails, and entrance margins. They describe architecture previews. A later
village connector apron can add depth, as it does for the regional Barracks;
reserve that addition explicitly instead of overwriting a porch or lobby.

| Region | Compact storehouse | Supply yard | Reason for the envelope |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Desert | 13 × 13 × 9 | 16 × 17 × 9 | Low masonry roof; retain the useful Plains room and court sizes |
| Savanna | 15 × 14 × 10 | 17 × 18 × 10 | Wider veranda, broad eaves, and posts outside the work routes |
| Taiga | 13 × 15 × 12 | 16 × 18 × 13 | Deeper entry shelter and a steep roof over the work wing |
| Snowy | 15 × 17 × 12 | 17 × 20 × 12 | Enclosed lobby with a real turn between doors; covered approach beside the court |
| Deep ocean / Tidal Towns | 16 × 16 × 11 | 18 × 18 × 11 | Raft underside, protected arrival deck, refuge space, and contained return passage |
| Jungle / Moog's Missing Villages | 15 × 15 × 12 | 17 × 18 × 12 | Raised floor, deep eaves, and a usable veranda below the roof |
| Swamp / Moog's Missing Villages | 15 × 15 × 11 | 17 × 18 × 11 | Solid raised floor, broad boardwalk landing, and a sheltered work edge |

The ordinary land variants start with full floor blocks at local Y=0 and
standing feet at Y=1. The three raised families reserve an underside at Y=0,
full floor blocks at Y=1, and feet at Y=2. These are authoring datums, not
world elevations or promises about water level. Draw the eventual connection
to the provider road separately. Foundations have finite depth.

The compact room normally keeps at least the Plains reference's 9 × 7 clear
interior, including its furniture footprints. Arrange the work bench near a
daylight opening, shelves along the opposite wall, and the Armory toward the
rear right. The yard normally keeps a 12 × 6 store room, a separate 3 × 3
covered working bay, and an open court center of at least 4 × 4. The ocean
yard can use an 11 × 6 store room with its wider exterior deck; its drawing
must still reserve the service area, cross-aisle, and two-cell interior bypass.
Keep crates at work-bay edges instead of filling the central delivery space.

## Desert: sandstone supply depot

**Character:** a pale, solid village depot with deep shade and a modest flat
roof. A shallow parapet, recessed entrance, and narrow high windows establish
its shape. Reserve visual thickness for corners and doorway reveals; ordinary
wall runs can remain one block thick to preserve the useful room.

**Palette:** smooth sandstone wall panels, cut sandstone corners and lintels,
ordinary sandstone at the base, sandstone slabs for roof and parapet trim.
Use stripped acacia for the loggia posts and a few timber headers, acacia doors
and shutters, and a restrained ochre terracotta band. Pave the working floor
with sandstone. A small yellow banner identifies the entrance.

### Compact storehouse

Retain the 9 × 7 room and rear service area of the Plains reference. Make the
front shelter a masonry entry recess, at least two blocks deep, with the door
pair visible from the road. Put the sentry on a shaded side of the landing.
Recesses and corner piers must sit outside the main two-cell approach.

Give the work bench a high two-cell window in the side wall. A second small
opening lights the shelf wall. Start the glazing above a solid sill; retain
shutters in the exterior margin. Use acacia shelves and a low stone-backed
work surface around the one crafting table. Exposed sandstone above the bench
and one timber header are enough interior detail; the ceiling stays simple.

Use a closed, flat slab-and-block roof with a half-block parapet on exposed
edges. End the parapet before the lower entry canopy meets the main wall.
Keep the parapet low enough to read as a roof edge. Reserve supported lantern
positions in the recess, over the main aisle, and near the supply area.

### Supply yard

Keep the rear storehouse and west workshop arrangement. Replace the timber
lean-to with a **loggia**, a roofed working edge carried by square masonry
piers and acacia headers. Its rear opening remains two blocks wide and joins
the sheltered store frontage. The 4 × 4 court center stays open to the sky.

Use pale paving, one low border, and a bench in the western shade. Put the
crafting table and decorative crate under the loggia; indoor shelving remains
at the rear. The main roof rises slightly above the loggia so the storehouse
reads clearly from the road. Use simple stepped lintels above open bays, with
all lower courses outside the required headroom.

**Detail and placement priorities:** concentrate darker sandstone at the base
and worn threshold, not random wall patches. Keep the apron solid when nearby
sand falls away. Check shade-roof seams and lantern supports. The flat roof
needs a later night and spawning check; its appearance alone proves neither.

## Savanna: acacia veranda storehouse

**Character:** a wide, welcoming work veranda beneath a low hipped or clipped
gable roof. Exposed posts and warm wall panels make the supply building feel
connected to the village's outdoor working spaces.

**Palette:** stripped acacia posts, acacia beams, planks, stairs, slabs, doors,
and shutters; warm terracotta infill with a few pale panels near the windows.
Use a restrained cobblestone base and coarse dirt or gravel beside solid stone
court paving. The main roof is acacia, with occasional unstripped acacia at
structural corners. Use an orange banner.

### Compact storehouse

Keep an enclosed 9 × 7 supply room behind a front veranda at least three
blocks deep. Extend the veranda toward the work-window side to create a
three-cell-wide side bay. This extra width gives the sentry and rest bench
their own frontage space. The direct door approach remains clear.

Use a low hip at the front and rear of the main roof, with its long ridge
running north–south. Broad eaves should visibly shade the wall panels without
lowering the room ceiling. Two normal framed windows admit daylight at the
bench and shelves. Keep upper infill closed; the occupied store remains a
sheltered room rather than an open pavilion.

### Supply yard

Widen the west working shelter into an open veranda with a 3 × 3 sorting bay
and a separate two-cell connection to the covered store frontage. The rear
storehouse gets an east–west clipped gable; the veranda has a lower sloping
roof in the same material. This gives the pair a shared character while
making their street silhouettes distinct.

Place posts at bay boundaries. Use a short side rail only at the rest bench;
keep the road opening and court-facing work bay open. The 4 × 4 court center
stays empty, with the crate and crafting table grouped in the western shade.
Reserve five supported lights across the arrival edge, workshop, frontage,
and rear room, following the Plains yard's distribution as a starting point.

**Detail and placement priorities:** show sun-faded panels and a darker base
course sparingly. Retain solid work paving through any gravel decoration.
The main check is the broad roof: posts, braces, and eaves must preserve the
veranda route in every rotation. Keep the bright acacia roof as the defining
material unless the rendered prototype shows that its scale overwhelms the
walls.

## Taiga: spruce equipment store

**Character:** a compact timber store with substantial joinery, a stone foot,
and a steep roof. Its longer entrance shelter feels useful in poor weather.

**Palette:** stripped spruce posts, spruce plank wall panels, unstripped log
corners, spruce roof stairs and slabs, and a cobblestone base with sparse
mossy cobblestone near the ground. Pale terracotta around selected windows
keeps the interior readable. Use spruce doors and shutters and a dark green
banner; keep dark oak to small trim if the roof needs separation.

### Compact storehouse

Keep the 9 × 7 room and use the additional depth for a covered entrance at
least three blocks deep. A projecting north–south gable shelters this porch.
Place the sentry beside the door approach under the eave and a short bench
against the front wall on the other side.

Use a pair of framed side windows at the indoor work area and shelf end. The
roof can show collar beams above the circulation clearance, with brackets
returning into wall posts. Use a timber floor over the stone base and a small
stone patch at the threshold. A chimney or live fire is unnecessary for an
equipment store; the heavy roof and warm lantern light provide its character.

### Supply yard

Put a steep east–west gable over the rear storehouse and an attached timber
work shed on the west. Close the shed's outer wall and its northern end while
leaving its court-facing side and rear connection usable. A covered frontage
joins the work bay to the store doors.

Use a solid stone court with gravel at its unused edges. Keep the center open
and the side shelter visually lower than the store roof. Put the crafting
table, bench, and a single decorative delivery crate under the shed. Keep
the rear store room's cross-aisle and eastern bypass separate from the service
area, as in Plains.

**Detail and placement priorities:** use a few repaired plank panels and
moss at the base. Check the longer eaves against rising ground and overhead
route clearance. Any applied snow is a later finishing choice confined to
roof or edge cells; it must not narrow the arrival lane.

## Snowy: winter supply store

**Character:** a solid store with pale infill, a steep spruce roof, and an
enclosed entry lobby. The lobby changes the floor plan and is the reason for
the larger depth. Weather protection is architectural; it adds no heating,
temperature, or snow-clearing system.

**Palette:** spruce framing and roof, white terracotta wall panels, and a
stone-brick/cobblestone base. Use spruce flooring and doors, small glazed
windows, and a muted blue banner. Snow can dress selected roof edges later;
keep ice and powder snow out of the floor, foundation, and routes.

### Compact storehouse

Use an 11 × 7 main room behind a **5 × 3 clear lobby**. Offset the outer and
inner door pairs by two cells, leaving a full three-row turn between them.
The lobby holds circulation only. Place the rest bench and equipment shelves
in the main room so the entrance cannot become a furniture bottleneck.

The following reservations establish that the proposed envelope can contain
the lobby. Coordinates are unrotated X/Z, with north at Z=0 and feet at Y=1;
ranges are inclusive. They are a drawing basis, not authored block states.

| Element | Compact reservation |
| --- | --- |
| Main room, including furniture zones | X=2..12, Z=8..14 |
| Outer doors | X=5..6, Z=3 |
| Clear lobby | X=5..9, Z=4..6 |
| Inner doors, in main front wall | X=7..8, Z=7 |
| Room aisle | X=7..8, Z=8..13 |
| Armory | (11,14) |
| Service area | X=10..12, Z=11..13 |
| Porch sentry | (10,2), separate from the approach |

Keep the lobby side walls at X=4 and X=10. The main shell occupies X=1..13,
Z=7..15, leaving an outer roof margin within 15 × 17. The shallow porch
covers the outer door landing and sentry. The main roof and lower lobby roof
need a closed junction; a beam across the lobby must clear its whole turn.

### Supply yard

Keep an open arrival court, sheltered west work bay, and rear supply room,
then insert the lobby between court and store. Low windbreak walls enclose
the unused court edges. Roof the workshop-to-lobby route while leaving a
4 × 4 central patch open to the sky. The result should read as a winter work
court, with sheltered daily work around an exposed delivery space.

| Element | Supply-yard reservation |
| --- | --- |
| Main room, including furniture zones | X=2..14, Z=12..17 |
| Outer lobby doors | X=6..7, Z=7 |
| Clear lobby | X=6..10, Z=8..10 |
| Inner doors, in main front wall | X=8..9, Z=11 |
| Interior cross-aisle | X=8..14, Z=12..13 |
| Armory | (11,17) |
| Service area | X=10..12, Z=14..16 |
| Interior eastern bypass | X=13..14, Z=12..17 |
| Open court center | X=8..11, Z=2..5 |
| Covered workshop-to-arrival crossing | X=2..7, Z=5..6 |
| Sentry | (12,2), in a covered side bay |

Cover both rows of the crossing through X=7 and connect them to the lobby
approach at X=6..7. Keep that roof west of X=8 at Z=5, leaving the court
center open. The main shell occupies X=1..15, Z=11..18. Keep its roof within
X=0..16, Z=10..19.
The west work bay retains its own 3 × 3 standing space beside the crossing.

**Detail and placement priorities:** draw both door pairs with actual hinges
and open-leaf positions before building the shell. Check native guard travel
through the offset lobby early. Protect the door landings from applied snow,
then test snowfall, light, and melt behavior in the packaged game. If door
traffic needs more room, revise the lobby drawing before adding decoration.
Occasional snow on the unused court edge is acceptable.

## Deep ocean / Tidal Towns: harbor supply raft

**Character:** a working harbor storehouse on a contained timber raft, with
visible underside beams, a sheltered landing, and a dry delivery deck. Use
ordinary wood to connect it visually with the village's floating roads.

**Palette:** oak deck planks and log raft beams, stripped spruce building
posts, oak wall panels, spruce roof and shutters, and oak or spruce rails.
Limit stone to small trim away from the raft underside. A cyan banner and
warm lanterns identify the landing. Rails and mooring posts provide maritime
detail without rope entities, boats, or a working crane.

### Compact storehouse

Place a 9 × 7 clear supply room toward the rear of the 16 × 16 platform.
Reserve a two-cell arrival lane from the road to its paired doors, a covered
crossing across the front deck, and a **3 × 3 refuge bay** beside the lane.
Use a modest north–south gable, a lower porch canopy, and two framed windows.
Keep the crafting table and shelves indoors.

Fence the deck perimeter except at the road connection. Keep the sentry on
the opposite side from the refuge bay so it cannot occupy the only step-aside
space. The room's clear aisle and front deck provide the return journey to
the same road entrance. There is no rear or side door opening onto water.

### Supply yard

Use the larger 18 × 18 raft for the rear storehouse, west sorting shelter,
and open delivery deck. Reserve a **two-cell passage along the eastern side**
inside the railing, connecting the arrival deck to a 3 × 3 rear refuge and
turning bay. It returns toward the front entrance; it is not a second road
connection. There is no promise of a loop behind the rear wall.

The store room can narrow to 11 × 6 clear cells to fit this exterior passage.
Keep a two-row cross-aisle inside its doors, the 3 × 3 service area, and a
two-cell interior bypass. Use an east–west rear gable, with a low sloping roof
over the west workshop and store frontage. Leave the central 4 × 4 deck open
to sky. Place the crafting table and delivery crate in the sheltered west
bay, away from the gangway.

**Entrance section:** reserve a full deck at the raised floor datum and a
two-cell half-step landing immediately before it. Match the final lower
landing to the observed road surface during integration. The existing
Barracks integration uses a half slab above Tidal Towns' unlit campfire roads;
that is a reference to measure against, not proof that these rafts connect.

**Detail and placement priorities:** continuous support under every route,
rail corner, sentry, and refuge comes first. Keep rails and posts out of the
landing and verify the rail ends against the adjoining road. Use short raft
beams beneath the deck; do not generate stilts to an unknown seabed. Test
water exposure and all rotations of the threshold. A failed road join needs
a landing correction, not an assumption that guards can swim to supplies.

## Jungle / Moog's Missing Villages: raised veranda depot

**Character:** a high, airy roof over a stone-footed timber depot, with deep
eaves and a generous shaded veranda. The roof has clipped ends and a visible
upper timber band. The occupied store stays enclosed and weather-sheltered.

**Palette:** jungle logs and stripped jungle posts, jungle plank wall panels
and floor, jungle roof stairs and slabs, and a restrained cobblestone/mossy
cobblestone plinth. Use jungle doors and shutters, a few pale terracotta
panels behind the bench, and a green banner. Keep bamboo to a possible small
decorative panel after the main wood palette has been reviewed.

### Compact storehouse

Keep a 9 × 7 room above the finite plinth. Put a three-block-deep veranda
across the front and retain a broad supported threshold between road and
deck. A tall north–south clipped gable and deep front eave distinguish it
from the Savanna store's lower roof.

Place the sentry at the veranda side and the short bench against the front
wall, outside the route. Use narrow upper glazed openings with fixed-looking
timber framing to suggest screened ventilation. Glass closes the openings;
ordinary decorative trapdoors remain interactive and need clearance in both
positions. The indoor bench gets a lower framed daylight window.

### Supply yard

Raise the court and workshop onto one solid work level. Use the rear store's
east–west clipped gable, a broad west veranda roof, and a covered frontage
between them. Keep at least 4 × 4 of the delivery court open to the sky,
surrounded by the taller roof and lower shelter.

The west bay holds the crafting table and decorative crate. Low rails protect
actual drops at the deck edge; the arrival lane and work-bay entrance remain
open. Carry the shared two-row interior cross-aisle and separate service
bypass through the rear room. Keep extra greenery outside those reservations.

**Detail and placement priorities:** use moss near the base and perhaps one
small planted-looking edge beyond the occupied deck. Reserve live vines and
leaves for a later growth and decay check, away from doors, eaves above routes,
and lamps. The raised threshold must meet the observed Moog's Missing Villages
(MMV) road elevation without a jump, ladder, or unsupported corner landing.

## Swamp / Moog's Missing Villages: boardwalk supply house

**Character:** a low, weathered storehouse with a substantial dry floor, dark
timber, mud-brick panels, and a covered boardwalk work edge. Lower roof lines
and smaller openings distinguish it from the Jungle pair.

**Palette:** dark oak posts, boards, roof, doors, and shutters; mud-brick wall
panels above a stone plinth; and occasional spruce repairs in deck edges or
shutters. Use sparse mossy cobblestone below the floor and a muted brown
banner. Mud bricks form walls and trim, while the principal walking surface
uses full planks. Water and wet-looking decoration stay outside that surface.

### Compact storehouse

Keep the 9 × 7 room on the raised floor, with a covered boardwalk landing at
least three blocks deep. Use a modest north–south gable extended into a lower
front canopy. The sentry stands to one side under the canopy, with the entry
lane and a three-cell turning bay kept clear.

Place a broad shuttered window above the work bench and a smaller opening at
the shelf wall. Use short wall-side shelves and a repaired plank rest bench.
Keep the Armory at the rear of the enclosed room. The solid floor continues
beneath every furniture position and wall opening; decorative gaps cannot
expose hidden water inside the building.

### Supply yard

Make the open court a raised plank working deck with a low west lean-to and
an enclosed rear storehouse. The rear roof runs east–west, visibly lower
than the Jungle equivalent. Close the workshop's outside wall with partial
mud-brick infill and dark timber posts, retaining its two-cell rear connection
to the sheltered frontage.

Keep a 4 × 4 open delivery area, the 3 × 3 covered sorting bay, and a wider
boardwalk landing at the entrance. Put the crate beside the crafting table
under cover. Use rails only at exposed outer drops, with continuous floor
support behind them. The interior service area and bypass follow the shared
yard arrangement.

**Detail and placement priorities:** concentrate repairs at deck edges and
door sills, with moisture wear at the plinth. Keep reeds and vines outside
the arrival and work routes. Supports stop within the template's finite
foundation; ordinary terrain still needs sampling. Check water and road
levels together, including the half-step entrance used as the MMV reference.

## Detail rules shared by the regional pairs

Use the [Plains architectural details](armory-architectural-details.md) as the
starting point for attachment and circulation reservations. Carry their
intent into each new roof rather than copying lamp coordinates blindly.

| Detail | Design requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Lighting | Start with three lights for compact stores and five for yards; reserve an additional lobby or remote deck light where the new plan needs it. Give every lantern a valid supporting face and keep the three-block route clearance. Measure darkness in game before fixing the final count. |
| Furniture | One crafting table; shelves, solid wooden crates, and slab/stair benches. Keep all furniture outside the service area, routes, door leaves, and duty cell. No additional inventories or profession blocks. |
| Windows | Give the work bench daylight and the supply room a second opening. Keep solid sills and wall backing for shutters; reserve both trapdoor positions. |
| Roofs | Close the store room and all shelter seams. Draw roof intersections as occupied blocks at their actual heights; overlapping X/Z footprints alone do not identify a conflict. |
| Signs of use | One small regional banner, concentrated base wear, a repaired panel, and grouped equipment silhouettes. Keep loose piles out of the court center. |
| Floors | Full, supported circulation and dry service cells; weathering changes materials without lowering random route cells. |

The current resupply behavior can withdraw ammo within four blocks and can
approach storage from several sides. A guard can resupply beside an exterior
wall. These designs preserve a usable entrance and service area; they do not
add front-face-only access, queueing, automatic snow removal, or swimming
behavior. Test ordinary resupply separately from a forced walking journey.

## Drawing and integration boundaries

The [drawing set](regional-armory-drawings.md) now provides dimensioned floor
plans, front and side elevations, main roof sections, and entrance sections
for every pair. Its schedule assigns door hinges, furniture, glazing,
shutters, beams, lamps, Armory anchors, and sentries. Snowy's entrance section
is unfolded through the offset doors; the raised entrances label the actual
provider-road elevation as unmeasured. The [furnished preview pack](regional-armory-preview.md)
now translates those schedules into native blocks for manual placement.

The current layout compiler limits each axis to 16 and volume to 4,096 blocks.
Several proposed envelopes exceed an axis limit. The largest architectural
envelope, the Snowy yard, is 4,080 blocks; adding a one-block connector apron
makes it 4,284. The direct integration writer authors this native template
without changing compiler validation or cutting the useful lobby. Its village
fit is checked separately in packaged Minecraft.

The [regional Barracks integration](regional-barracks-integration.md) also
provides provider-specific connection references. Its land/MMV street-end
strategy and Tidal road-end strategy also serve the Armories, with separate
fit trials for their footprints. The current inventory of 25 built-ins plus 16 regional Barracks
uses 41 definition slots. The [regional Armory integration](regional-armory-integration.md)
adds sixteen entries, including Plains, for 57 total. The runtime cap is now
96 across twenty-four pools, including room for the custom village adapters. The integration writer authors native
NBT directly and leaves the schematic compiler limits unchanged. Preview
drawings and templates require no such entries.

Before eventual release, use the packaged mod and provider jars in an ordinary
Forge installation. Check four rotations, doors and attachments, native
walking through the complete entrance, ordinary resupply, save/reload stock,
night lighting, weather, and naturally joined roads. The current Plains
evidence is separate from the regional preview trials.

Required corrections are blocked or unsupported approaches, unusable service
space, unsafe occupied deck cells, broken attachments or rotation, invalid
definitions, and stock duplication. Occasional cosmetic terrain seams,
foundation exposure, unused-edge snow, and rare crowding can remain follow-up
work. These designs do not require perfect terrain generation to be useful.
