# Regional Barracks design proposals

Status: working design brief, September 9, 2026. These are proposals for the
compact and courtyard Barracks in eight settings. The two furnished Plains
prototypes are references; the remaining designs have not been implemented.
Dimensions, materials, and room arrangements below are starting decisions for
discussion, not validated structure templates. The subsequent
[drawing review](barracks-drawing-review.md) develops all sixteen layouts and
records the current geometric revisions. The
[architectural details](barracks-detail-design.md) specify the next level of
openings, furnishings, attachments, and roof construction.

The goal is a recognizable family of village guardhouses whose architecture
changes with the climate and settlement. Roof shape, entrance treatment, room
arrangement, and foundations should distinguish the regions even without their
colors. Each region should eventually give the Village Armory, Village
Watchtower, Sentry Post, and Training Yard the same architectural vocabulary.

## Shared identity and layout requirements

Both layouts serve the same Barracks role. The compact layout concentrates
circulation indoors. The courtyard layout uses a court and sheltered walks to
connect its rooms. A courtyard needs a usable outdoor center; a decorative gap
between roofs is not enough.

| Element | Proposed common requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Occupancy | Four decorative sleeping places; preserve the existing maximum of four possible initial guards and current difficulty/population rules. |
| Interior functions | Sleeping spaces, shared eating/planning table, work surface, wall-side equipment decoration, and clear assembly space. |
| Duty positions | Three assembly slots and one entrance sentry, authored separately for each geometry. Do not put the stationary sentry in the main entrance lane. |
| Main movement | Two clear blocks across principal doors and passages; three full air blocks above ordinary routes. Paired wooden doors remain two blocks tall. |
| Turning space | Prefer a clear 3 × 3 landing where a main route changes direction or divides between rooms. |
| Courtyard | Aim for at least a clear 4 × 4 open-air center in addition to the covered circulation. Mark this separately from the gross court area. |
| Secondary route | A second ground exit where it can land safely. On water, a protected internal loop and refuge deck take priority over an exit into the water. |
| Floor | Full blocks under guard assembly, sentry, and principal circulation cells. Resolve road-height transitions outside the standing areas. |
| Roof | One primary mass and a subordinate wing or porch; finish both ends and the roof junction. No occupied upper floor in the first design set. |
| Identity | Modest banner by the entrance, consistent guardhouse proportions, visible entrance shelter, and practical furnishings. Regional banner colors are accents. |
| Lighting | Normal supported lanterns in each occupied room and at the entrance. Start with 3–4 for compact and 4–5 for courtyard, then choose actual counts from the floor plan and night trial. |
| Services | Carpet bedrolls, decorative equipment shelves, and one crafting table. Functional beds, containers, Village Armory access, medical stations, and new rewards are separate decisions. |

Shared dimensions are movement targets, not proof that a casualty carrier, a
group of guards, or a gun can use the space. Those require later packaged-game
trials. Door swings, rails, stairs, shutters, beams, and lanterns all count when
checking clearance.

## Regional overview

Dimensions are **width × depth × total height**, in blocks, including roofs,
approaches, and any support structure inside the template. They are proposed
envelopes, not exact floor plans. Raised designs must budget their underfloor
space within the height; deck height and road connection are not fixed yet.

| Setting | Architectural direction | Compact envelope | Courtyard envelope | Most visible distinction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Plains | Timber and plaster village guardhouse | 15 × 13 × 12 | 16 × 16 × 12 | Unequal gables and a welcoming porch |
| Desert | Sandstone guardhouse with sheltered loggias | 13 × 13 × 9 | 16 × 16 × 10 | Low parapets, recessed openings, and deep shade |
| Savanna | Acacia lodge with broad verandas | 15 × 13 × 10 | 16 × 16 × 10 | Low, wide roofs and open shaded frontage |
| Taiga | Spruce forest lodge | 13 × 15 × 12 | 16 × 16 × 13 | Long proportions, heavy beams, and steep roofs |
| Snowy | Enclosed winter guardhouse | 15 × 14 × 12 | 16 × 16 × 13 | Sheltered entry lobby and a wind-protected court |
| Deep ocean / Tidal Towns | Floating harbor guardhouse | 16 × 15 × 10 | 16 × 16 × 11 | Timber raft, guarded gangway, and protected deck |
| Jungle / MMV | Jungle timber lodge on a stone base | 15 × 14 × 12 | 16 × 16 × 12 | Deep eaves, screened openings, and a raised veranda |
| Swamp / MMV | Weathered marsh guardhouse | 15 × 13 × 11 | 16 × 16 × 12 | Dark timber, mud brick, stone plinths, and boardwalks |

## Plains

**Character.** An established rural guardhouse: substantial enough to be a civic
building, with the scale and warmth of neighboring houses. The existing
[Plains prototypes](barracks-designs.md) provide the starting geometry.

**Palette.** Cobblestone and andesite foundations; stripped oak posts and beams;
white terracotta wall panels; oak floors; spruce roof planes with restrained
dark-oak edging. Use moss only near damp ground contact. Green is the banner and
textile accent.

**Compact — Village Guardhouse.** Keep the lower sleeping wing beside the
taller common hall, with the porch marking the hall entrance. Four bedrolls
form two pairs in the quiet wing. Put the common table near the front windows,
the work surface beside the entry, and the assembly space toward the rear.
Retain the east-side exit and the two-block connection between wings. Refine
the roof seam, shelf placement, and furniture proportions before treating the
current prototype as final.

**Courtyard — Village Guard Court.** Keep the L-shaped west sleeping wing and
rear common hall. Two sleeping bays connect internally as well as through the
covered walk. The court gets a worn stone-and-earth surface, low boundaries,
and an open three-block entrance. Keep its center empty; a noticeboard or small
equipment bench belongs against the sheltered wall. The sentry stands beside
the entrance, away from arrivals and the hall doorway.

**Details to retain.** Shutters, small upper gable windows, visible ceiling
beams, a modest banner, and a warm entrance light. Avoid adding a tower or tall
perimeter wall that would obscure the ordinary village-building identity.

**Next design decision.** Confirm whether to retain the existing open side
exit or give it paired doors. Check the actual open court area against the new
4 × 4 target before freezing the courtyard plan.

## Desert

**Character.** A thick-looking sandstone building arranged around shade. Wall
depth comes from recessed doors, reveals, and corner piers; do not make every
wall two blocks thick at the expense of movement space.

**Palette.** Cut and smooth sandstone walls; regular sandstone foundations;
limited terracotta bands; stripped acacia lintels, doors, and furniture. Use
light sandstone slabs for roof edges and parapets. Yellow or ochre textiles
provide the small color accent.

**Compact — Sandstone Guardhouse.** Replace the twin gables with two low roof
volumes at different heights. A recessed, shaded entrance leads into a common
room; the sleeping room sits to one side behind a broad interior opening.
Use small high windows and deep external reveals, keeping the central passage
open. Furnish the common room with a wall-side table and stone bench; arrange
four bedrolls along the sleeping room's cooler outer edges. A rear or side arch
provides the second supported exit. Keep the flat roof decorative initially.

**Courtyard — Shaded Guard Court.** Use two enclosed wings and an additional
arcaded shade edge, subject to the 16-block envelope. A low perimeter wall
encloses the remaining court edge. Recess the entrance within the wall and
keep a clear route across the court to the common hall. The sleeping wing has
two pairs of bedrolls; dining and equipment benches sit under the loggia or
inside the rear hall. Use a pale full-block court surface with a restrained
terracotta border. A water-jar-like decorative grouping belongs in a corner;
do not put a fountain in the movement space.

**Distinctive details.** Stepped parapets, shade slits, timber lintels, shallow
wall niches, and simple geometric trim. No towers or crenellations are needed
to communicate the guardhouse role.

**Next design decision.** Choose between a fully plaster-like smooth-sandstone
finish and exposed cut sandstone with pale trim. Recommend the latter for the
main walls, with smooth sandstone emphasizing entrances and roof edges.

## Savanna

**Character.** A broad, airy lodge with deep shade and a strong horizontal
silhouette. Its roof should look different from the Plains and Taiga gables.

**Palette.** Acacia logs and stripped acacia frames; warm terracotta infill;
acacia planks and stairs for the main roof; stone footings and plank verandas.
Use dark acacia bark at important corners. Orange or muted yellow textiles
support the wood color without covering every wall in bright panels.

**Compact — Acacia Guard Lodge.** Use a low hipped or clipped-gable roof over a
wider common room, with a narrow porch roof extending the shaded frontage.
Place the common table along the veranda-facing wall and split the rear rest
space into two bedroll alcoves. Use open-looking screened window bays above
solid lower walls. The main route runs between the entrance and a supported
side exit, with the assembly area in a wider bay off that route.

**Courtyard — Veranda Guard Court.** Arrange two low wings around a sunlit
court, joined by a broad shaded veranda. The common room can have a wide
opening onto the veranda, while the sleeping bays stay enclosed. Make the
court a mix of full coarse dirt and stone with a deliberate worn route. Keep
roof posts at the outside edges of the walks. A low fence and gate opening
mark the frontage; the entrance sentry occupies a shaded side bay.

**Distinctive details.** Broad eaves, acacia screens, low roof hips, woven-looking
trapdoor panels, and long benches. Avoid copying Desert parapets or filling
the courtyard with a large acacia tree.

**Next design decision.** Compare a shallow hip against a clipped gable in an
elevation sketch. Recommend the shallow hip if it leaves enough ceiling space
and can fit its overhangs inside the compact envelope.

## Taiga

**Character.** A durable forest lodge built from heavy timber on a stone base.
The compact version is longer than it is wide, with a deeper entry shelter.

**Palette.** Spruce logs and stripped spruce; spruce plank walls; cobblestone
and restrained mossy cobblestone at the base; spruce roof planes with limited
dark-oak edge detail. Use green or brown cloth and pale bedroll pillows.

**Compact — Forest Watch Lodge.** Put a steep main roof over a long common
hall and sleeping section, with a small lower porch roof at the front. Two
sleeping alcoves at the back hold two bedrolls each. The table sits along one
side of the front hall; equipment pegs, a bench, and decorative wood storage
sit against the opposite wall. Keep a clear central route and a side exit near
the rear. Exposed rafters and a stone hearth-like alcove make the interior feel
inhabited; a working fire is a separate choice.

**Courtyard — Forest Guard Yard.** Join a sleeping lodge and common hall around
a compact working yard. Use a timber boundary and roofed entrance rather than
a fortress wall. Covered walks shelter the doors; the rear hall's taller roof
remains visible over the lower wing. Put wood and equipment decoration under
the eaves, leaving the central yard and all turns clear. Keep the entry sentry
inside the sheltered frontage with a clear view of the approach.

**Distinctive details.** Log ends, stone plinths, pronounced roof pitch, deep
door frames, and sheltered equipment benches. The principal difference from
Snowy is its working-yard layout and more open entry.

**Next design decision.** Choose whole-log walls or framed plank panels.
Recommend framed panels, with whole logs concentrated at corners and roof
supports, to avoid a visually solid dark box.

## Snowy

**Character.** A winter guardhouse that keeps normal movement sheltered. Snow
changes the entrance, courtyard treatment, and interior arrangement as well as
the palette.

**Palette.** Spruce framing, pale terracotta panels, a substantial stone base,
dark spruce roofs, and red textile accents. Place snow on selected exterior
roof surfaces and exposed edge details. Main walking floors use full wood or
stone blocks; ice and loose snow layers are outside their intended routes.

**Compact — Winter Guardhouse.** Add an entry lobby with a clear 3 × 3 turning
area before the main hall. Offset the interior opening so the front door does
not frame a straight view through the whole sleeping space. Keep four bedrolls
in two rear alcoves, a shared table beside the brighter window, and a cloak/
equipment bench near the lobby. Use a steep roof and short eaves that leave
doors unobstructed. The secondary exit gets a small covered landing.

**Courtyard — Winter Guard Court.** Enclose the two inhabited wings and connect
their entrances with continuous covered circulation. Timber wind screens close
most of the exposed court edges; a roofed gate lobby admits arrivals. Preserve
a small open-air court in the middle, with full-block paving on the route and
snow confined to decorative edge areas. A sentry alcove looks through the
entrance without occupying its turn. The court should feel protected while
remaining visibly outdoors.

**Distinctive details.** Entry lobbies, smaller windows, red bedroll accents,
mantel-like stonework, cloak pegs, and selective roof snow. A large icicle
display or exposed ice terrace would distract from the practical building.

**Next design decision.** Prefer a wind-protected courtyard over a completely
glazed atrium for this first pair. Test later snowfall and door use before
claiming routes remain clear in weather.

## Deep ocean — Tidal Towns

**Character.** A harbor guardhouse built into a floating timber settlement.
“Deep ocean” describes the settlement location; the Barracks belongs above the
water on a deck, not underwater or on a tower reaching the seabed.

The author's [Tidal Towns page](https://modrinth.com/datapack/tidal-towns)
describes floating villages in vanilla deep ocean. The inspected 1.3.4 jar uses
oak, spruce slabs and trapdoors, logs, and unlit campfires in its connected
platforms. Its road walking surfaces are not uniformly full blocks. This
provides a material reference, not an already validated guard connection.

**Palette.** Oak log raft members and stripped oak framing; oak cabin walls;
spruce floors, roof planes, edge battens, and rails; small cyan/white banner
accents. Keep masonry to a few fittings. Use timber shapes for buoyancy
decoration; do not add functional barrels as a side effect of the visual theme.

**Compact — Harbor Guardhouse.** Place a low cabin on a compact raft, with a
three-block-deep arrival deck and a sheltered sentry bay beside the gangway.
Put the common table near the front cabin window and arrange four bedrolls in
two aft alcoves. A protected side walk can connect the front and rear decks
if there is room after accounting for rail collision. Give the rear a small
refuge landing rather than an unguarded exit into open water. Keep the roof
low enough to read as a light timber cabin.

**Courtyard — Harbor Guard Deck.** Put a bunk cabin and a common-room cabin
around a shared dry deck. The deck serves as the courtyard; do not cut an open
water well into its center. Covered edge walks connect the cabins. One broad
gangway joins the settlement, with a full-floor assembly area just inside it.
The water-facing perimeter gets continuous rails, with deliberately positioned
view openings only where guard and gun clearance can both be demonstrated.

**Distinctive details.** Visible raft beams, battens, sheltered lanterns,
nautical banner colors, and a clearly legible gangway. A mast, ship hull,
submerged room, or seabed foundation would be a separate layout concept.

**Next design decision.** Fix the deck/road connection before choosing exact
interior coordinates. The shipped house's entrance finalizes to an unlit
campfire; directly replacing that with the current Barracks' air connector
does not establish matching walking height. Require a level or tested stepped
transition. Until a second safe connection is supported, use one gangway and
a protected circulation loop instead of two nominal exits.

## Jungle — Moog's Missing Villages

**Character.** A ventilated timber-and-stone lodge with deep rain shelter.
Make it part of the ground-level settlement first. Tree platforms and
ladder-dependent access would require a separate design and movement strategy.

[MMV's description](https://modrinth.com/mod/mmv-moogs-missing-villages)
sets a vanilla village direction. The inspected 2.1.1 jungle homes principally
use jungle wood, jungle stairs/planks, cobblestone, and mossy stone, with vines,
leaves, trapdoors, and occasional bamboo. Use that material family as the base;
an all-bamboo building would be a stronger departure.

**Palette.** Jungle wood and stripped jungle framing; jungle plank roof planes;
cobblestone and small mossy stone patches at the base; jungle trapdoor screens;
limited bamboo-block or mosaic panels. Use green cloth and brown bedrolls.
Keep living vegetation outside entrances, guard positions, and firing lanes.

**Compact — Jungle Guard Lodge.** Raise the occupied floor slightly on a short
stone plinth or short supports, with a wide, shallow entrance stair where the
actual road height requires it. Use a generous veranda and steep roof with
deep eaves. Put the common room at the ventilated front, two sleeping alcoves
at the rear, and equipment shelves against a dry internal wall. A supported
side landing provides the second exit. Windows use solid lower panels with
screened or glazed upper openings.

**Courtyard — Jungle Guard Court.** Two sheltered wings surround a dry central
yard. Join them with a roofed veranda and give each sleeping bay direct access
to the sheltered circulation. Use full plank or stone routes; depict drainage
along external edges instead of an open channel across the court. Keep foliage
to one exterior corner or outside the template's movement envelope. The common
table sits near the court-facing wall, with the assembly space behind it.

**Distinctive details.** Deep eaves, timber screens, a stone base, a few bamboo
panels, and carefully contained moss. Avoid a temple silhouette, giant tree
trunk through the hall, hanging bridge, or ladder-only sleeping area.

**Next design decision.** Recommend jungle wood and stone as the dominant
palette, with bamboo as an accent. Choose the amount of raised floor only
after measuring MMV's street-to-house transitions.

## Swamp — Moog's Missing Villages

**Character.** A weathered but maintained marsh guardhouse. It should feel like
part of MMV's village, with deliberate damp-weather construction and a clearly
safe place to stand.

The inspected 2.1.1 swamp homes emphasize dark oak, cobblestone, mud brick,
packed mud, spruce, and moss. The shipped biome tag directly includes normal
swamp and optional modded swamp tags; it does not explicitly list vanilla
mangrove swamp. A mangrove-dominant palette is therefore an optional future
interpretation, not the starting match for this installation.

**Palette.** Dark-oak posts, stairs, and roof trim; spruce floorboards; mud-brick
wall panels and selected roof sections; cobblestone and mossy stone plinths.
Use moss and cracked masonry near water contact, with repaired timber above.
Muted purple or green cloth provides the small entrance accent.

**Compact — Marsh Guardhouse.** Use a slightly raised dry floor over low stone
supports, with a covered boardwalk approach. A steep, modestly irregular roof
protects the entry and side landing. Arrange the common room at the front,
four bedrolls in two drier rear bays, and equipment shelves above the floor
along an internal wall. Keep underfloor openings decorative and inaccessible
from the principal route. A second exit is appropriate only where its landing
connects to solid ground or a protected deck.

**Courtyard — Marsh Guard Court.** Two wings surround a broad dry timber deck,
with short masonry supports where the site allows them. Covered walks connect
the rooms. Rails protect water-facing edges; low stone boundaries can face
dry ground. Keep an unobstructed assembly square in the center. Put wet-weather
equipment decoration under the eaves, with the table and bedrolls inside the
enclosed rooms. Roots, reeds, and water remain outside the usable deck.

**Distinctive details.** Dark framing, mud-brick panels, small repaired roof
patches, moss at the supports, and sheltered boardwalk junctions. The building
is occupied and maintained; broken floors, dangling bridges, and interior
root tangles do not belong on its main routes.

**Next design decision.** Recommend dark oak and mud brick for the first pair.
Choose the foundation strategy from actual dry-ground and shallow-water sites;
do not promise a stilt template can accommodate arbitrary water depth.

## Placement and authoring work to plan separately

These constraints affect which drawings can become templates. They are recorded
here for the next planning pass; no implementation is included in this brief.

| Area | Current evidence | Required design/implementation decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regional selection | The source compiler currently accepts five vanilla region keys. Runtime entries can name target pools. | Separate architectural style from the installed settlement's pool/connector rules, then add explicit ocean, jungle, and swamp authoring support. |
| MMV entrances | Both inspected regional road families target `mmv:building_entrance`. | Author matching connectors; sending a vanilla `minecraft:building_entrance` into the correct MMV pool is insufficient. |
| Tidal entrances | `joshie:village/ocean/houses` connects to `joshie:village/ocean/roads`; inspected entrance floors use partial-height campfires. | Resolve connector position, walking elevation, transition geometry, and full support under all guard cells. |
| Metadata versions | The inspected jar uses `joshie:village_ocean`; the public page's locate example uses a different ordering. | Follow the installed resource IDs, and keep public art references separate from version-specific integration contracts. |
| Geometry bounds | Current compiler limits each axis to 16 blocks and volume to 4,096. | Include porches, roof edges, rails, stairs, and underfloor supports in the envelope. Reduce an overfull layout rather than silently removing movement space. |
| Entry count | Current implementation limits generated/runtime entry inventories. Sixteen Barracks layouts plus 25 legacy templates would be 41 entries before further pool mappings. | Audit total entries before extending the complete building family to eight settings. Reused art mapped to extra pools can still cost extra entries. |
| Generation weights | Each current layout entry adds its own relative weight. | Choose family-level selection and layout proportions before release; do not assume two weight-1 layouts preserve the current Barracks frequency. |
| Foundations | Rigid placement and an empty processor list do not grow stilts or extend foundations to the seabed. | Specify allowed ground/water conditions, finite supports, and when to reject a site. |
| Secondary connections | Current source layouts require one road jigsaw. | Keep secondary land openings distinct from real road joins; plan additional joins deliberately if a later ocean design needs them. |
| Regional modifiers | Snow cover, vines, moisture, and canopy clearance can change a route after placement. | Trial the relevant weather/growth conditions and record where decorative blocks must stay clear. |

Reference code: [layout authoring](village-building-layouts.md),
[template classification](village-template-support.md), and
[layout source validation](../gradle/village-building-layouts.gradle).
Mod-specific palettes and connector examples above come from the installed
Tidal Towns 1.3.4 and Moog's Missing Villages 2.1.1 jars. They are a resource
inspection, not new in-game compatibility tests.

## Drawing package for each proposed layout

The [drawing review](barracks-drawing-review.md) now supplies furnished plans,
roof footprints, mass elevations, and schematic entrance sections for all
sixteen layouts. The list below remains the target for the completed design
package; exact facade details, door swings, and connector placement are pending.

Before implementing a regional pair, produce the following reviewable design
material. Define the visual choices first; resolve exact block coordinates once
the regional direction is accepted.

1. A palette board naming actual Minecraft blocks and their intended role.
2. A front and side elevation showing roof pitch, overhangs, entrance depth,
   windows, rails, supports, and approximate finished height.
3. A furnished floor plan separating furniture, sleeping spaces, assembly
   cells, sentry, principal routes, door swings, and clear turning spaces.
4. A roof plan showing every roof intersection and the uncovered court area.
5. A foundation/entrance section showing the intended road, floor, water, and
   roof elevations. This comes first for ocean and other raised layouts.
6. Exact anchor, connector, guard-cell, lantern, and duty-slot coordinates,
   with each approach checked against the completed drawing.
7. A review list for later normal Forge tests using packaged jars: rotations,
   native attachment and collision, group passage, live gun sightlines, night
   light, and natural placement in the intended settlement.

The first drawing pass uses cut sandstone with smooth trim, shallow Savanna
hips, an enclosed Snowy gate lobby, and timber Ocean rafts. Review these
provisional choices and the revised floor envelopes before specifying the
individual facade blocks and exact road connections.
